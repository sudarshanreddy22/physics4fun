Hot or Cold?

Activity 7.1 • Sense of touch

  • Keep the right hand in warm water and the left hand in ice-cold water for 1–2 minutes.
  • Place both hands together in tap water.
  • The same tap water may feel cool to one hand and warm to the other.
  • Conclusion: Our sense of touch is not a reliable thermometer.

Temperature and Thermometers

Section 7.2

  • Temperature is a reliable measure of hotness or coldness.
  • A hotter body has a higher temperature than a colder body.
  • Clinical thermometers measure human body temperature.
  • Laboratory thermometers measure temperatures for other purposes.

Digital Clinical Thermometer

Section 7.3.1 • Body temperature

  • A digital clinical thermometer uses a heat sensor and battery.
  • Wash the tip, reset the instrument, place it under the tongue and wait for the beep.
  • Read the display; clean and dry the tip after use.
  • Do not immerse the digital display in water or hold the instrument by its tip.

Body Temperature

Normal temperature and variation

  • The average normal human body temperature is 37.0 °C, equal to 98.6 °F.
  • Healthy people's readings may vary with age, time of day and activity.
  • An armpit reading may be about 0.5–1 °C lower than actual body temperature.
  • Infrared thermometers can measure temperature without touching the body.

Temperature Scales

Celsius • Fahrenheit • Kelvin

  • Celsius scale: degree Celsius (°C). Fahrenheit scale: degree Fahrenheit (°F).
  • Kelvin scale: kelvin (K), the SI unit of temperature.
  • Kelvin = Celsius + 273.15; for example, 27 °C = 300.15 K.
  • Write a space between the value and unit. Do not use the degree symbol with K.

Laboratory Thermometer

Section 7.3.2 • Parts and range

  • A laboratory thermometer has a sealed glass tube, liquid-filled bulb and narrow liquid column.
  • The column rises or falls with a change in temperature.
  • The textbook example has a range from −10 °C to 110 °C.
  • Laboratory thermometers may contain coloured alcohol or mercury; handle carefully.

Range and Smallest Division

Activities 7.3 and 7.4

  • Range = lowest temperature to highest temperature marked on the instrument.
  • Smallest division = difference between two numbered marks ÷ number of equal small divisions.
  • Example: 10 °C between marks ÷ 10 divisions = 1 °C per division.
  • Always check the actual thermometer: its range and divisions may differ.

Measuring Water Temperature

Activity 7.5 • Correct method

  • Hold the laboratory thermometer vertically in the water.
  • Keep the bulb immersed without touching the bottom or sides of the beaker.
  • Wait until the liquid column stops rising; read at eye level.
  • Take the reading while the thermometer remains immersed.

Ice, Boiling Water and Observations

Teacher demonstration • Activity 7.6

  • Temperature remains constant while ice is melting or water is boiling under unchanged conditions.
  • In Shillong, students recorded boiling-water readings between 97.8 °C and 98.1 °C.
  • Differences can arise if correct reading procedures are not followed.
  • Boiling-water experiments must be performed under teacher supervision.

Air Temperature and Weather

Section 7.3.3 • Activity 7.7

  • Room thermometers give an approximate room temperature.
  • Weather reports show daily maximum and minimum air temperatures.
  • Record both temperatures for 10 successive days and compare changes.
  • Anna Mani (1918–2001) developed weather-measurement instruments in India.

Chapter 7 • Summary
