Light - Introduction

Light - Introduction
  • Light is a form of energy, which causes a sensation of vision in our eyes.
  • Light can travel in vacuum with a speed of 3 × 108 m/s.
  • Properties of Light are given below.
Rectilinear Propagation
Light travels in a straight line.
Reflection
Light bounces back from a surface.
Refraction
Light bends on entering another medium.
Dispersion
White light splits into colours.
Scattering
Light spreads in many directions.
Interference
Light waves overlap and combine.
Polarization
Vibrations are restricted to one plane.
Diffraction
Light bends around edges/openings.

Reflection of Light

Reflection of Light
The bouncing back of light into the same medium is called reflection of light.

Due to reflection, objects in our surroundings become visible to us.

Incident Ray
Ray of light incident on the reflecting surface.
Reflected Ray
Ray of light reflected from the reflecting surface.
Angle of reflection (r)
Angle made by the reflected ray with the normal.
Angle of incidence (i)
Angle made by the incident ray with the normal.
Normal
Imaginary line drawn perpendicular to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence.

Laws of Reflection

Laws of Reflection
I
Law: The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection: i = r.
II
Law: The incident ray, the normal and the reflected ray at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.
Laws of Reflection hold for both plane mirrors and spherical mirrors. For normal incidence, i = 0° and r = 0°.

Refractive Index — Meaning & Key Points — 1

Refractive Index — Meaning & Key Points — 1
  • The extent of change in direction when light travels from one medium to another is expressed in terms of Refractive Index.
  • It has no units — it is a dimensionless quantity.
  • Higher refractive index → lower speed of light in that medium.
  • It depends on: Nature of the medium and Wavelength of light used.
  • Two types: Absolute Refractive Index and Relative Refractive Index.
Absolute Refractive Index n

Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to the speed of light in that medium.

n=cv
Relative Refractive Index n21

For Medium-2 with respect to Medium-1:

n21=n2n1=v1v2

Power of a Lens (P)

Power of a Lens (P)
  • The ability of a lens to converge or diverge light rays depends on its focal length.
  • The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length.
P=1fwhen f in m
P=100fwhen f in cm
1D=1m−1

P = power of lens

f = focal length

+Positive power — Convex (converging) lens

−Negative power — Concave (diverging) lens

Remember: Power is inversely proportional to focal length. Example: a lens of focal length 0.5 m has power +2 D; a convex lens of focal length 0.25 m has power +4 D.
