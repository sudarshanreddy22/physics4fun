Reflection of Light

Laws of reflection

The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r). The incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.

Spherical Mirrors

Concave and convex mirrors

A concave mirror has an inward-curved reflecting surface; a convex mirror has an outward-curved reflecting surface. Pole (P), centre of curvature (C), principal focus (F), and radius of curvature (R) describe the mirror.

Mirror Terminology

Focal length and radius

For a spherical mirror with a small aperture: R = 2f. The principal axis passes through the pole and centre of curvature.

Concave Mirror – Image Formation

Object positions

Beyond C: real, inverted, diminished, between C and F. At C: real, inverted, same size, at C. Between C and F: real, inverted, enlarged, beyond C. Between F and P: virtual, erect, enlarged, behind the mirror.

Convex Mirror – Image Formation

Always virtual and erect

For a real object placed in front of a convex mirror, the image is virtual, erect and diminished, formed between P and F behind the mirror.

Mirror Formula and Magnification

Cartesian sign convention

Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u. Magnification: m = hᵢ/hₒ = −v/u. Use signed distances measured from the pole.

Refraction of Light

Change of direction

Light changes direction when it passes obliquely between transparent media of different optical densities because its speed changes. The frequency remains unchanged.

Laws of Refraction

Snell’s law

The incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in the same plane. For a given pair of media, sin i / sin r is constant. Refractive index n₂₁ = sin i / sin r = v₁/v₂.

Refraction through a Glass Slab

Lateral displacement

The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray for a rectangular glass slab with parallel faces, but it is laterally displaced.

Spherical Lenses

Convex and concave lenses

A convex lens converges parallel rays towards its principal focus. A concave lens diverges parallel rays, which appear to come from its principal focus.

Convex Lens – Image Formation

Object position determines the image

Beyond 2F₁: real, inverted, diminished. At 2F₁: real, inverted, same size. Between F₁ and 2F₁: real, inverted, enlarged. Within F₁: virtual, erect, enlarged.

Concave Lens – Image Formation

Virtual and diminished

A concave lens forms a virtual, erect and diminished image between its optical centre and principal focus on the same side as the object.

Lens Formula and Magnification

Cartesian sign convention

Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u. Magnification: m = hᵢ/hₒ = v/u.

Power of a Lens

Unit: dioptre (D)

P = 1/f, where f is in metres. Convex lenses have positive power and concave lenses have negative power. For lenses in contact, P = P₁ + P₂.
